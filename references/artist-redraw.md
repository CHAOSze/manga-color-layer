# Complete movable parts and review them

Choose extraction with completion for trustworthy contours, whole-part redraw when patch repairs repeatedly create seams, or a hybrid. Do not enforce pixel equality at the cost of coherent artwork. Preserve expression, proportions, silhouette, ink and screentones perceptually. A supplied reference PSD guides construction, not style replacement.

For each part record its visible boundary, parent movement, rigid/flexible regions, occluders and surfaces that motion will reveal. Complete base color, shadows, linework and texture across the attachment boundary. Size overlap from planned translation, rotation and bending plus a margin; a filled hole alone is insufficient. Do not clip completed artwork back to the original visible alpha.

Before coloring or segmentation, resolve ambiguous anatomy and materials by tracing their contours through the source drawing: a rounded silhouette may be shoulder, folded sleeve, or limb; a narrow band may be hair or costume trim. Record disputed ownership explicitly and apply user corrections to the actual masks and artwork, not only layer names. Plan front/back occlusion separately from material: rear hair goes below the face, bangs above it. Export and inspect these separately; a single full hair cutout cannot represent both depth positions. Do not restore broad source patches into completed layers without reviewing ownership, because this can reintroduce gold trim into hair or hair ink into skin.

Group parts that share motion when their depth order and deformation remain compatible. If a split repeatedly causes gaps, doubled contours or implausible contact and independent movement is not required, merge the connected regions into one editable plate. Flattening layers does not repair inherited cuts: inspect the merged plate itself at enlarged joints, fill notches, remove false flaps and redraw continuous folds before accepting it. Repair any static seam before merging; do not preserve a failing split just to keep more layers. Rebuild parents, pivots, motion previews, manifests and import scripts after changing the grouping. A shared plate may use local deformation with attachment constraints; a painted boundary is not automatically a layer boundary. Split where relative motion, replacement, articulation or occlusion requires it. A single image can contain rigid and flexible regions; multiple control points do not automatically require multiple layers. Conversely, a merge that prevents required movement is inappropriate even if it simplifies completion.

Before adding a split, name the independent control it enables and weigh that benefit against new seams, reconstruction and synchronization burden. Prefer the fewest coherent movable plates that meet the requested shot. Do not subdivide an otherwise useful complete part merely to represent every depth change or improve pixel equality. If restrained local deformation and stable contacts suffice, retain that part as one plate. Respect a user-approved coarse grouping while preserving explicitly requested facial controls; never turn one example's part count into a universal template.

Respect stated silhouette and occlusion priorities. Perceptual fidelity is not permission to reroute a complete part around a neighboring object or reshape its tips. If the user accepts that object being covered, preserve the part's flow and let the overlap occur; use whole-part placement/scale when requested instead of unrequested local warping. Do not trade a preserved form for accessory visibility the user did not require.

Choose attachment treatment from the current geometry. Flexible strands, membranes or fabric need stable roots and continuous surfaces; articulated joints need sufficient backing and consistent contact. Held or attached objects can share a layer with their support when no relative motion is needed, but must remain separate if movement, replacement or changing depth order requires it. Inspect every exposed contour and remove unrelated fragments introduced by reconstruction.

Color thresholds suggest material membership but do not define complete physical occluders. Include their ink, antialiasing, highlights and interior details; a porous skin-only or dark-only cover can leak newly painted material as speckles during motion. Use structural contours and inspect both signs of motion. A grasp or attachment is a physical pin: complete its hidden overlap, hold the contact stable, and deform the free length. When a continuous part occupies several depth planes around a rigid object, share its deformation and attachment constraints across those planes. Do not use whole-layer translation as the only test for a part anchored at multiple contacts, or claim an unconstrained range from a constrained preview.

Register cropped edits using recorded crop offsets and reliable landmarks. For strong downscaling, area-prefilter premultiplied color/alpha before registration and inspect fine ink continuity; direct point sampling can turn continuous strokes into dashes. Crossfading differently shaped extractions and redraws can introduce shoulders and interrupted ink; prefer a coherent whole-strip redraw or a targeted join repaint where this occurs. Use significant alpha bounds rather than faint noise for scaling; inspect fallback transforms visually. Broad copying from the original can transfer hair onto skin or ornaments onto sleeves. Apply source restoration only to reviewed ownership regions.

For foreground outlines/shadows left on a backing, duplicated feature marks or weak attachment joins, follow [backing-cleanup.md](backing-cleanup.md). Audit all intersecting plates; cleaning the face base alone does not remove a hair fragment retained by an eye frame. Preserve approved expression marks without protecting neighboring foreign ink as part of the same source patch.

## Rear/front ownership and completion
A hidden backing completes the rear surface; it must not contain another copy of the foreground bangs, cheek locks, curls or their tip outlines. Paint coherent rear flow across occluded regions. Preserve necessary internal underlap for motion, but distinguish that from duplicated recognizable foreground geometry. Inspect the rear layer alone with foreground hidden, then test front-only, rear-only and opposing movement; moving both identically can conceal duplicates. Register edits using unchanged visible landmarks, not newly invented hidden texture. Recheck the exterior silhouette after registration.

Boundary completion must extend the actual material and strand direction across the cut, including linework, shadow and texture. Do not copy the foreground's alpha or tip outline onto the backing: this recreates its silhouette even if its colors were changed. Inspect a narrow reveal strip around each moving boundary on contrasting backgrounds at both motion extremes. Avoid hard rectangular patch ends, repeated ink contours and abrupt tone transitions; feather only local color joins, not structural ink edges. A smooth hidden rear mass should read as rear anatomy, not as a recolored second set of bangs.

## Attachment and silhouette checks
Resolve material from contour continuity, folds, seams and attachment points, not the apparent limb-shaped silhouette or a previous generated color. Close-fitting sleeves, gloves and finger-anchored garments can resemble bare limbs. Follow fabric to its actual anchor; preserve exposed digits separately in the coloring and constrain garment motion at both anchors when needed.

Inspect every extracted skin/cloth interface with the neighbor hidden. Remove foreign fold ink, thin wedges and clipped stubs from the skin plate; complete a smooth anatomical backing beneath the garment. Preserve visible contact contours on their owning layer, and check overlap as well as the assembled seam.

Validate the assembled silhouette after registration, especially shared roots and nested contours. Inset a hidden backing where the source places it under a foreground rim; do not let a reconstructed crown, joint or attachment protrude beyond its source envelope at rest or under intended motion. Choose this relationship from the source rather than assuming all rear hair must always be lower.

## Review gates

Follow [review-gates.md](review-gates.md) in order. During coloring compare the drawing to the coloring; during separation compare the colored master to the exported rest assembly. Inspect isolated completed surfaces as part of static review. Resolve the visible differences and hidden-boundary defects before small-range independent/combined/opposing motion checks. A repair after motion returns to static review. Keep intermediate evidence internal unless requested, and report an actual pass/fail result rather than merely saying the image was reviewed.
