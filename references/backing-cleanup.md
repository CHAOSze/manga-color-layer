# Clean backings and repair attachment surfaces

Use when a part looks acceptable assembled but exposes old outlines, shadows, cuts or duplicated details during relative movement. These are diagnostic and repair methods, not a guarantee that a previous attempted repair succeeded. Preserve approved expression, visible anatomy and strand direction; cleanup is not permission to redesign them.

## Diagnose ownership before painting

Inspect the approved coloring, the occluder alone, and every underlying plate separately. List each suspect mark and its owner: actual anatomical contour, expression feature, occluding-part outline, contact/cast shadow, or extraction artifact. Similar color is not shared ownership: a brow and hair can have the same hue. Check all plates that intersect the boundary, including facial support layers, rather than cleaning only the layer named body or face.

## Clean a face or other underlying material

1. Hide the foreground and independent features. Look for retained fringe lines/tips, dotted ink, colored slivers, triangular shadow remnants, clipped blush/hatching, and duplicated feature marks. Distinguish these from real ears, folds, creases or expression lines.
2. Identify which approved marks must remain and where they are actually visible. Protect the real brow shape, gaze, lid contours, mouth corners, nose and distinctive marks as appropriate. Remove accidental duplicates while keeping the real mark. Do not preserve an entire source crop or a broad protection polygon merely because it contains a valid feature; it may also contain the foreground outline being removed.
3. Repaint a continuous backing through the area that movement can expose, including an overlap margin. Continue skin/material color, shading, hatching and texture across the old cut. Remove the foreign ink and its shadow/color footprint, not just its darkest pixels. For a moving cast shadow, decide how it follows its caster; leaving the original static shadow behind can reveal the separation.
4. Register a local painted completion against reliable landmarks. Preserve the approved exterior anatomy where sound. Blend material joins in color/texture support, without blurring defining ink or making opaque material translucent. Large skin stickers, hard patch borders, copying adjacent colors or alpha expansion alone do not establish a complete painted surface.
5. Check overlapping feature plates as well. Old hair ink can survive in a lid/tear frame or skin support even after the face base is clean. Restore legitimate hidden feature surfaces there, while retaining the approved visible eye/expression. Recheck iris, sclera and mouth ownership after changing an occluder; an old alpha mask is not anatomical evidence.
6. Hide the features again, then reassemble. Verify that the cleaned backing contains no unwanted feature copies and that the restoration has not pasted hair, duplicate sweat/marks or old shadows back onto it. If ownership is uncertain, compare source contours and repair locally rather than trusting hue thresholds or a nominal layer name.

## Repair continuous hair or flexible attachments

Inspect the join at native scale and in context. One connected component is insufficient: a large lock joined by a thin thread, a sharp notch, or a surviving accessory-shaped cut may still break or kink under Puppet deformation. Paint a material connection with plausible width, strand flow, ink, shadow and texture. Do not create an artificial bridge just to pass connectivity checks, or clip the completion back to the former visible alpha.

Preserve the approved visible flow and tips. Respect the user's occlusion priority: if an accessory may be covered, do not reroute or reshape hair to reveal it. When a split's motion benefit does not justify the boundary cost, simplify the grouping. Merging damaged fragments alone does not fix their inherited seams; inspect the merged plate itself.

For a rear surface hidden by foreground hair or a face, infer its own anatomy and rearward/downward flow from the source. Do not copy the foreground's recognizable bangs, cheek curls, tip outlines, alpha silhouette or boundary strokes onto it. Recoloring those copies does not make them rear geometry. Preserve genuine exposed rear locks and their direction; a quiet hidden scalp may differ internally from the foreground. Judge crown overlap and exterior extent from the current subject, not a universal height rule.

## Verify the repair, not just the procedure

- Solo each affected plate on contrasting mattes: complete material, meaningful attachment width, clean ownership, no foreign fragments or interior alpha holes.
- Compare the actual saved assembly to the approved coloring and approved expression. Allow necessary local underlap changes; reject unrequested hairstyle, brow/eye or pose changes.
- Move the occluder and backing independently and in opposition through the intended small range. When root motion is intended, test relative root translation/rotation as well as tip bending. Inspect revealed face/temple/cheek, accessory and attachment strips for old ink, duplicate shapes, hard seams and broken joins.
- Treat component counts, opacity audits, numerical differences and generated previews as supporting evidence. They cannot prove natural painting or deformation. Distinguish local raster previews from an executed AE test.

Record symptom, owner, attempted repair, reviewed evidence and remaining defect. User-reported remaining problems reopen the relevant review gate; do not describe the attempted method or its output as a validated success. Preserve confirmed improvements while leaving unresolved results explicitly pending.

## Constrain completion from visible evidence

Before generating missing rear hair, trace the approved colored master's genuinely exposed rear-hair silhouette, tips, bounding extent and attachment landmarks in shared canvas coordinates. Supply these as a spatial guide along with the obscuring parts and required motion margin. Preserve or register trustworthy visible paint and extend only the hidden surface along posterior strand flow; do not let a generic generated wig replace a larger or differently shaped visible lock. Compare coverage of every exposed reference lock after registration. A quiet scalp behind the head does not compensate for missing lower or side hair.

At neck, ear, headphone, collar and hair-root junctions, hide each neighbor separately. Inspect for transparent slits, clipped anatomy, mismatched shadows, severed material and accessory-shaped cuts. Paint the actual supporting surface continuously through the joint; color adjustment or merging the layers alone cannot fill missing artwork. Keep neckline/contact shading consistent with the approved coloring. If relative motion has little value and reliable separation cannot be made, retain a continuous combined surface.

Compare both ears against the original pose and approved coloring. Correct accidental cropping, scaling or missing lobes; account for head tilt, perspective and occlusion before deciding whether size asymmetry is wrong. Do not enforce numerical symmetry on an asymmetric view.

## Registration and alpha cleanup pitfalls

Identify registration markers by expected location and connected component as well as color. Remove only those confirmed marker components; a global color threshold can also erase legitimate pink accessories or other palette-matching material. Inspect the registered part immediately, including accessory rims and contact joins.

Audit the whole nonzero-alpha extent against contrasting mattes. A test that starts at alpha 80 or 128 can overlook weak but visible generator debris, marker remnants and black speckles outside the silhouette. Retain genuine narrow antialiasing close to painted contours; remove isolated weak-alpha background contamination without expanding or eroding the actual artwork.

Do not paste sparse source RGB strokes over a differently registered redraw merely to claim source fidelity. Misaligned linework produces doubled/broken strokes and hard transition seams even when alpha stays continuous. Use source paint only where geometry and material agree; otherwise retain coherent local redraw and review its actual visible contours against the master. Check every visible lock's tip placement and reach, not just the overall hair bounding box.
